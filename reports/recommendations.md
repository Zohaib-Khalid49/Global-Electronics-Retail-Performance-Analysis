# Business Findings and Recommendations

## Executive Summary

The analysis shows strong growth through 2019, followed by a significant decline in 2020. Computers is the largest category, the United States is the largest customer market, and the online channel is an important source of revenue. The business should protect its strongest segments while investigating the causes of the 2020 decline, product-level margin differences, and concentration among high-value customers.

The recommendations below are based on the cleaned sales data and the outputs in the `reports/` directory. They are intended to guide management decisions and follow-up analysis, not to establish causal explanations that the dataset cannot prove.

## Revenue declined sharply after 2019

### Business question

How did revenue change over time, and which period requires management attention?

### Evidence

- Annual revenue increased from **$6.95M in 2016** to **$18.26M in 2019**.
- Revenue declined to **$9.29M in 2020**.
- **December 2019** was the highest month in the available monthly report, with **$2.48M revenue**, **1,250 orders**, and **9,343 units**.
- The monthly trend shows a sustained decline after the early 2020 peak.

### Interpretation

The business had built substantial momentum before 2020, but the decline indicates a major change in demand, channel activity, product mix, store operations, or data coverage. The pattern is a management priority because it affects both revenue planning and inventory decisions.

### Recommendation

Conduct a recovery review by comparing 2019 and 2020 performance by month, country, store, online channel, category, and product. Management should identify where the decline was concentrated, review inventory and promotional decisions during the affected months, and use the findings to create a channel-specific recovery plan.

### Limitation

The data shows that revenue declined but does not establish why. External events, including the COVID-19 pandemic, may have contributed, but this dataset does not contain enough information to confirm causation. The final year is also partial, ending in February 2021.

## Computers is the largest revenue category

### Business question

Which product categories contribute most to revenue and gross profit?

### Evidence

- Computers generated **$19.30M revenue**, representing **34.62% of total revenue**.
- Computers generated **$11.28M gross profit**.
- Home Appliances was the second-largest category, with **$10.80M revenue** and **$6.30M gross profit**.
- Computers had a gross margin of **58.43%**, close to the overall margin of **58.58%**.

### Interpretation

Computers is the portfolio's largest commercial engine. Its scale means that changes in computer demand, pricing, availability, or product mix could materially affect total business performance.

### Recommendation

Protect availability and customer demand in the Computers category while reviewing performance at subcategory and product level. Management should prioritize high-profit products, monitor stock availability, and assess whether promotions are increasing profitable sales rather than only increasing volume.

### Limitation

The analysis does not include inventory levels, stockouts, discounts, returns, marketing spend, or operating costs. Category revenue and gross profit therefore do not prove that Computers is the most efficient category or the best area for additional investment.

## Some categories and products have lower margins

### Business question

Where may the business have opportunities to improve gross profitability?

### Evidence

- Overall gross margin is **58.58%**.
- Cell phones had a category margin of **56.58%**.
- Games and Toys had the lowest category margin in the report at **54.73%**.
- Several individual products in the product report have margins near **49.0%**.
- The analysis identifies **Product 444** as a leading gross-profit contributor, with approximately **$337,986 gross profit**.

### Interpretation

High revenue does not automatically mean high profitability. Lower-margin products may be reducing the return generated by strong sales volume, while high-profit products may provide opportunities for more profitable assortment and promotion decisions.

### Recommendation

Review product-level prices, supplier costs, promotions, and sales volumes for products below the overall margin. Test targeted price or sourcing changes, and use gross profit—not revenue alone—when selecting products for promotions, assortment expansion, and sales incentives.

### Limitation

The calculated gross margin uses listed unit prices and listed unit costs. It does not account for discounts, rebates, returns, taxes, shipping, inventory write-offs, or operating expenses. These results should therefore be treated as gross-margin indicators rather than final product profitability.

## The United States and online channel are major contributors

### Business question

Which markets and sales channels should receive management attention?

### Evidence

- The United States generated **$29.87M revenue**, **$17.49M gross profit**, and **14,221 orders** in the customer-country report.
- The online channel generated **$11.40M revenue**, **$6.67M gross profit**, and **5,580 orders** in the store-performance report.
- Store 55 was the highest-revenue physical store in the report, with **$1.42M revenue** and **$830,551.75 gross profit**.

### Interpretation

The United States is the most important geographic market in the available customer analysis, while online sales represent a substantial channel that should be managed separately from physical stores. The strongest physical store may provide useful operational insight, but it should not be treated as a direct benchmark without considering its local context.

### Recommendation

Protect service levels and customer retention in the United States, and analyze online performance as a separate channel. Compare online and physical channels using revenue, gross profit, order volume, delivery performance, acquisition cost, and operating cost where available. Investigate the practices and product mix associated with the strongest physical stores.

### Limitation

The dataset does not provide complete channel costs, marketing spend, foot traffic, store-level operating expenses, or delivery performance for all records. Revenue leadership therefore does not prove that a market or channel has the highest return on investment.

## Revenue is concentrated among high-value customers

### Business question

How dependent is revenue on the most active customers, and where could retention activity have the greatest value?

### Evidence

- The dataset contains **15,266 customer records**.
- **11,887 customers** had recorded sales.
- The top **10% of active customers contributed 35.98% of revenue**.
- The top 100 customers contributed **5.53% of revenue**.

### Interpretation

A relatively small group of active customers contributes a meaningful share of revenue. Losing these customers could have a disproportionate effect, while increasing activity among less-engaged customers may reduce dependence on the highest-value group.

### Recommendation

Create a customer-retention program for high-value customers, including monitoring of purchase recency, frequency, and value. At the same time, identify customers with occasional purchases and test targeted cross-sell, replenishment, or loyalty campaigns to broaden the revenue base.

### Limitation

The available data supports historical revenue concentration analysis but does not provide customer acquisition cost, churn, profitability after service costs, or future customer lifetime value. Retention actions should be tested against incremental profit, not revenue alone.
